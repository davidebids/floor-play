# Design brief

## Player promise
A living garden pond appears under the player's hands and knees. It looks convincing while idle and responds immediately enough to invite exploration.

Feeling: curiosity, calm and delight. Primary verb: approach/chase fish. Secondary verbs: crawl, reach, pat and splash. Every5–30seconds, approach a fish or move a hand, see a local response, then follow a returning fish. Across1–5minutes, individual koi vary their paths and speeds while the pond remains coherent. There is deliberately no score, timer, failure, pressure or punishment for this early-childhood play activity.

## Core loop
The player moves toward fish or makes hand motions; local fish avoidance and water feedback reveal the connection; fish gradually return and invite another interaction. The consequence is in world state, not a UI reward. Parent controls pause, blank, tune and recover the session.

## Spatial plan
Target6x4ft top-down composition, configurable aspect. Irregular stone edge and natural vegetation frame generous clear central play water. Floating lily groups provide scale/landmarks and shadows. Fish remain within the water, turn smoothly ahead of banks, and respond locally rather than globally scattering.

## Art direction
Natural clear shallow garden pond, soft daylight, photographic material detail. Bed, bank, water, caustics/refraction/reflections, vegetation, fish appearance, animation, and effects are all first-class quality surfaces. The projector image is judged as a full environment, not isolated fish. Imagegen supplies suitable raster assets; live code supplies water, lighting, deformation, swimming, and interaction.

## Acceptance boundary
Approximate hand-strike/hover interaction is acceptable. Human physical contact is not measured. Scene quality must be reviewed in motion. Browser/GPU evidence does not establish floor brightness, projector thermal/mounting suitability, real-world latency, or toddler tracking.
